# Dr. Bancroft — Marketing Angles v3

**Merges** Claude's Synthesis v2 (1,350 sources, 2,784 verbatim quotes) with GPT's Deep Research Synthesis v2 (customer journey, evidence ladder, ad-test design).
**Date:** 24 September 2026.
**References:** `VOC-####` and `DB-####` refer to the corpus files. "GPT [n]" refers to a reference in GPT's document, which hasn't been re-checked here.

**Offer assumptions, taken from GPT's document and not confirmed:**
- a $54.99 bundle of five 5oz bars (25oz)
- five scents, including tallow and pine tar
- square bars
- lavender and peppermint fade in storage; cedarwood is mild
- target CPA of about $32
- ad budget of about $85/day

Where an angle depends on one of these, it's marked **[offer]**.

---

## 1. The frame

**Umbrella position: "Measured, not marketed." The soap with the numbers on it.**

- **Why it works:** it answers the category's biggest unmet need. People can't believe what soap brands say:
  - "Doesn't live up to the adverts." (VOC-2565)
  - "Everything claims to be gentle." (VOC-1260)
  - Hype-versus-reality complaints appear in 52 quotes across 20 communities.
- **Nobody else does it.** None of the 160 competitor pages in the corpus publishes test numbers.
- **It's defensible.** A tested claim survived a lawsuit: "a test, even if flawed, is still a test" (DB-0696).

**GPT's evidence ladder decides how strong each angle's copy can be:**

| Level | What it is | Can run... |
|---|---|---|
| **L1** | Facts: bar count, weights, scent names, ingredients, price, refund terms | Now |
| **L2** | Visible demonstration: real bars, real lather, real packaging | Now, if filmed in ordinary conditions |
| **L3** | Reported experience from real trial users | After a user panel |
| **L4** | Measured or comparative claims: showers per bar, tightness score, scent over time | After in-house tests |

Every angle below names its current level and what it needs to move up.

---

## 2. Angles, ranked

The ranking weighs:
- strength and spread of the evidence (after the adversarial review)
- fit with the five-bar bundle
- how hard the angle is for competitors to copy
- whether it can run now

### A1. "Five bars. Five scents. Here's exactly what you get." (offer clarity) [offer]

- **Runs at:** L1, now.
- **Insight:** soap is hard to judge online. People can't smell it, and "wish I could have smelled it first" (VOC-2451). A five-bar bundle at $54.99 can look like one expensive bar. GPT's first hypothesis is that clarity about the assortment increases qualified clicks.
- **Evidence:** Squatch's 8-bar pack at Target (GPT [44]); Bend's and Bronner's bundles (DB-0470, GPT [45]); buyers do price-per-bar maths: "$15 for a 6-pack compared to $5 a bar at Walmart" (VOC-1617).
- **Hooks:**
  - "5 bars. 5 scents. 25 oz. One box."
  - "Find your scent without buying five brands."
  - "Your shower, stocked for the next [N] months."
- **Visual:** all five real bars with their boxes, a scent card for each, and the weight shown. No single-bar hero shot.
- **Watch out for:** fear of paying for scents they'll dislike. Pair it with the guarantee terms (A9).
- **What it tests:** whether clarity about the offer lifts qualified CTR and purchases.

### A2. "Count the showers, not the bars." (value per use)

- **Runs at:** L2 now. L4 once the wear test is done.
- **Insight:** customers judge soap by cost per month, not price per bar.
  - Accepted: about $1–4 a month. "$8, but it lasts me for 3 or 4 months" (VOC-0046).
  - Rejected: about $14–30 a month. "these bars only last a week. Meaning you would spend $416/year on body soap." (VOC-2669)
  - Dr. Squatch's lowest Target sub-score is value (3.76–4.33, DB-1329 to DB-1334).
- **[offer] The maths:** at $11 a bar, if one bar lasts a month, that's about $11 a month. That's between the accepted and rejected ranges. **This angle only works if a bar is measured to last clearly longer than a month.**
- **Hooks:**
  - "Still here on day seven."
  - "The box shouldn't smell better than the soap." (VOC-2730)
  - "Cost per month, printed on the box."
- **Visual:** a time-lapse of bar life on a draining dish; the bar weighed on day 1 and day 30; hands versus washcloth.
- **Watch out for:**
  - Bar life varies hugely with usage. "I dunno how you people are scrubbing but every bar lasts me at least a month" (VOC-0459). The six-day bar in VOC-2531 was "placed back in the box" after each shower. **Disclose the method.**
  - Compare only against premium bars. Against $2 Dove the maths loses (VOC-2550).
- **What it tests:** whether measured bar life matters to the target buyer (GPT's third hypothesis).

### A3. "Scent, rated." (honest scent)

- **Runs at:** L1 and L2 now, for the names, the notes and the smell on opening. L4 once the panel has rated scent over time.
- **Insight:** scent is the category's biggest source of love (126 positive quotes). It also fails in two opposite ways:
  - fading (45 quotes): "as soon as you towel dry the smell is completely gone" (VOC-2546)
  - overpowering or artificial (45 quotes): "Smells like kitchen floor cleaner." (VOC-2525)
- **Scent happens at three moments,** each worth claiming separately (GPT §3.1):
  1. the dry bar on opening
  2. while lathering
  3. on skin afterwards
- **Hooks:**
  - "It smells like what it says." (answers VOC-2532, "doesn't smell like coffee. Zero...")
  - "Rated for the shower. Rated for after."
  - "Big in the shower. Clean skin after." (lean into it being a shower scent: VOC-0516)
- **Visual:** the scent card showing notes and strength when dry, in the shower and at towel-dry; the actual bar lathering.
- **[offer] Watch out for:**
  - **Don't feature lavender or peppermint in scent-strength creative until the storage test confirms they hold up.** Otherwise the ad promises what the aged bar can't deliver.
  - Pine tar is strong and polarising (VOC-0192 vs VOC-2362). Label it strong rather than hiding it.
  - Never claim "all day" scent.
- **What it tests:** which moment of scent is the real buying trigger (GPT's second hypothesis).

### A4. "Clean. Not stripped. Here's the number." (skin feel)

- **Runs at:** L2 as a question now ("That tight feeling after a shower?"). L3 and L4 only after a panel.
- **Insight:** it's a real need (189 quotes from 141 sources): "This clean feeling is what makes your face feel dry and tight 2 minutes later." (VOC-1042). Winning language: "super clean without that annoying squeak feeling" (VOC-1221).
- **It's a parity claim, though:**
  - Customers already credit Squatch with it: "without drying it out" (VOC-2572).
  - Beekman claims it (DB-0436).
  - The science doesn't favour true soap here. Alkaline soap raised skin water loss more than other formulations in comparative studies (GPT [9–11]; DB-0340, DB-0779).
  - So **it wins only with a published panel score, compared against a syndet bar.**
- **Hooks:**
  - "The two-minute test: towel off, wait, touch your face."
  - "Super clean. No squeak."
  - Once tested: "[X]% felt no tightness at 30 minutes."
- **Never say:**
  - "Real soap won't dry you out like detergent bars."
  - "Moisturizing" as a headline.
  - Anything about eczema.
- **What it tests:** whether the concern gets attention, and whether buyers report comfort afterwards. If it gets clicks but buyers don't report comfort, the product doesn't match the promise.

### A5. "Read the back first." (ingredients without fear)

- **Runs at:** L1, now.
- **Insight:**
  - 130 quotes show people reading ingredient lists.
  - "Natural" helps a product get considered (29 positive vs 16 sceptical), but vague claims backfire.
  - The classic gotcha is "parfum" in a "natural" bar (VOC-2431).
  - Fear of chemicals gets mocked: "Poison Ivy and Belladonna are all natural" (VOC-0252).
- **Hooks:**
  - "[N] ingredients. Here's what each one does."
  - "Every word on the label, explained."
  - "We picked these for a reason."
- **Visual:** a hand turns the bar over and each ingredient gets a plain-English line.
- **Never say:** "chemical-free", "non-toxic", or unqualified "natural" (DB-0690, DB-0694).
- **[offer] Tallow:** name it plainly. It's more loved than opposed in the corpus ("Tallow = superior lather." VOC-0171), but people distrust where it comes from: "Most brands can't actually tell you where their tallow comes from" (VOC-0264). Stating the source is a hook in itself.
- **What it tests:** whether a specific ingredient story beats generic "natural".

### A6. "Bought by her, loved by him." (gifting and household)

- **Runs at:** L1 and L2, now.
- **Insight:**
  - About 25–30 reviews on 15+ retail sites are from women and parents buying for men.
  - "my wife buys me 12 bars every Christmas" (VOC-0618).
  - "Definitely recommend for a stocking stuffer!" (VOC-2667).
  - Partners also veto scents: "Since I value my wife, I will probably not order this one again." (VOC-2348)
  - **[offer] A five-scent box is a natural gift format.**
- **Hooks:**
  - "Five scents. Let him pick a favorite."
  - "A gift that doesn't reduce him to a stereotype." (answers VOC-1544)
  - "Approved by the person next to you in bed."
- **Timing:** Q4 and Father's Day.
- **Watch out for:** sexualising anyone. And avoid seeming to target women generally unless that's deliberate (see §4 on CPM).

### A7. "Soap for the shower. Scent for the day."

- **Runs at:** L1, once a leave-on product exists.
- **Insight:** customers already solve scent fade by buying a leave-on product: "I use Dr Squatch too but it doesn't last. I did buy their oils and that sticks." (VOC-0581). Others split the jobs: "Bar to actually cleanse, body wash to smell good." (VOC-0318)
- **Use:** a later extension. It turns the fade complaint into an upsell instead of a promise on the soap itself.

### A8. "Same bar. Every batch." (consistency)

- **Runs at:** L1, now, as a policy.
- **Insight:**
  - Reformulation and shrinkflation anger long-time users: "Dove should be ashamed…" (VOC-1841); "you've lost a customer of 20 years." (VOC-1725)
  - Retiring a scent strands people: "Mistake to retire this one…" (VOC-2555)
- **Use:** a trust and retention page (formula version, weight printed on the bar, core scents never retired), more than a lead acquisition angle.
- **[offer] Watch out for:** the batch-variation issues GPT notes (brittleness, scent unevenness) must be fixed first. This promise can't run if batches differ.

### A9. "Pick your favorite. We'll cover the rest." (risk reversal)

- **Runs at:** L1, once the terms exist.
- **Insight:**
  - The biggest fear about the bundle is paying for scents they'll dislike (GPT §2.1).
  - Bend offers a 90-day guarantee and a free travel bar (DB-0497).
  - Quote evidence is thin (about 8 quotes), so treat this as an offer mechanism, not a stand-alone angle.
- **Hooks:**
  - "Don't love a scent? Tell us which one."
  - Clear refund terms on the product page. A vague badge doesn't count.

### A10. "Real lather, rated grit" / "Built for a hand"

- **Runs at:** L2, now.
- **Insight:**
  - Lather has 142 positive quotes: "lathers life cool whip" (VOC-2412).
  - Grit is net positive (about 20 positive vs about 7 negative) but must be expected: "like 60-grit sandpaper" (VOC-0114).
  - **[offer] Square bars:** complaints about sharp brick edges and slipping appear in 31 quotes across 20 communities: "will hurt your foot when dropped" (VOC-2115). Test grip before featuring the shape. Rounded corners are safer.
- **Use:** as demo footage inside A2 and A3, rather than as a lead angle.

### Always-on search

- **Target:** searches like "Dr Squatch alternative" and similar terms, answered with the numbers and no disparagement.
- **Evidence:** people are asking (VOC-0373, VOC-0528).
- **Don't size it as a segment.** Explicit defection is about 11 quotes, mostly from r/DrSquatch.

### Dropped or demoted

- **Body odor / "still smell after showering" / deodorizing.** The evidence is thin (13 quotes), and the claims would move the product into cosmetic or drug territory (GPT §3.3; DB-0390, DB-0391).
- **"Real soap vs synthetic detergent" attack.** It gets mocked (VOC-0760) and the science doesn't support superiority (GPT [9–11]). Say "traditional soap made from [fats] and lye" as a fact only.
- **Hard water.** Keep it to lather and film, and test before claiming. There's no SWET result in the corpus.
- **Masculinity-led copy ("real men").** Treat this as a tone rule: never insult the buyer (VOC-0765). Humour is fine.
- **Fragrance-free.** Keep it for a future product line, not the current five scented bars. Only 6 quotes come from men's channels.

## 3. What to test first

This follows GPT's design for about $85/day: three clearly different concepts, each in two executions. That's six ads in one campaign, with one message per ad so a result actually teaches something.

| Concept | Execution A | Execution B | Primary read | Kill / scale signal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Offer clarity (A1)** | Static: five bars with scent cards, weights and price | 15-second unboxing of the real box | CTR to landing page → add-to-cart | Needs about 2–3× CPA in spend before judging purchases. Judge on CTR and add-to-cart earlier. |
| **2. Scent, rated (A3)** | Scent-card static (dry / shower / after) | Lather video of the two strongest scents (not lavender or peppermint) | Thumb-stop and CTR; which scent gets clicks | Same |
| **3. Value per use (A2)** | "Count the showers" static using the measured number, or a weigh-in demo if no number yet | 30-day bar time-lapse | CTR and conversion rate vs concept 1 | Same |

**Second wave:** A4 (only once a panel number or real trial quotes exist), A5, and A6 timed for Q4.

**GPT's cautions to keep:**
- Don't kill an ad after about $5 of spend because of its CPM.
- CPM is the price of reaching people, not a measure of demand.
- Before blaming the message, pull the age, gender and placement breakdowns and the landing-page data.
- If the ads are delivered mostly to women, that could come from how Meta classifies the ad, or it could be the gift buyer. A6 tests which.

## 4. Claim guardrails

| OK now (L1 and L2) | Only after testing (L3 and L4) | Never |
|---|---|---|
| Bar count, weights, scent names and notes, ingredients, price, refund terms, "traditional soap made from [fats] and lye", real demo footage | Showers per bar, cost per month, "[X]% no tightness", scent strength after the shower, "lathers in hard water", user quotes | "Natural" or "all-natural" unqualified, "chemical-free", "non-toxic", "moisturizing" as the headline, "pH-balanced", "hypoallergenic", eczema/itch/acne, antibacterial or "kills odor", "all-day scent", "detergents damage your skin", comparisons against named competitors without data |

Any scent claim makes the product a cosmetic under FDA rules (MoCRA registration and listing). Confirm with counsel.

---

## 5. What would re-rank these angles

- **The storage test shows lavender and peppermint fade:** reformulate those two, or keep them out of all scent-led creative.
- **The wear test shows under a month per bar:** demote A2, and lead with A1 and A3.
- **A panel shows clear comfort against a syndet:** promote A4 to the lead angle. It's the most emotional angle, and it becomes defensible.
- **Delivery skews to women and converts:** move budget to A6.
